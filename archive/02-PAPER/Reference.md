## Hebbian learning

Hebbian learning is a fundamental concept in neuroscience and neural network theory, often summarized by the adage "neurons that fire together, wire together" [10]. It refers to a type of associative learning where the simultaneous activation of cells leads to pronounced increases in synaptic strength between those cells. This concept is named after Donald Hebb, who introduced the idea in his 1949 book "The Organization of Behavior" [10].

### **Key Characteristics of Hebbian Learning:**

1. **Synaptic Plasticity**: Hebbian learning is a primary mechanism of synaptic plasticity, where the efficiency of synaptic transmission is altered based on the activity of neurons. It's considered a biological basis for learning and memory in the brain.
2. **Co-activation of Neurons**: The core principle of Hebbian learning is that if a neuron takes part in firing another neuron, the connection between these two neurons is strengthened. This is often referred to as 'activity-dependent synaptic strengthening.'
3. **Long-Term Potentiation (LTP)**: In neuroscience, LTP is often cited as a cellular and molecular example of Hebbian learning [14]. LTP involves the strengthening of synapses based on recent patterns of activity. These changes can result in an increase in signal transmission between two neurons.
4. **Simple Associative Learning**: Hebbian learning is considered a mechanism behind simple associative learning processes like classical conditioning. It enables the formation of associations between different stimuli or between a stimulus and a response.
5. **Self-Organizing Networks**: In artificial neural networks, Hebbian learning rules can lead to the development of self-organizing networks that adaptively change their structure based on input patterns.
6. **Limitations and Extensions**: While Hebbian learning provides a foundational learning rule, it is not without limitations. For instance, it doesn't inherently account for 'forgetting' or reduction in synaptic strength (often addressed by anti-Hebbian mechanisms). Moreover, pure Hebbian learning does not consider error-correction mechanisms, which are central to many other forms of learning in neural networks.
7. **Applications**: Hebbian learning principles have been applied in various fields, including computational neuroscience, psychology, artificial intelligence, and machine learning, particularly in unsupervised learning scenarios.

In summary, Hebbian learning represents a foundational concept in understanding how neural connections strengthen through use and experience, forming the basis for learning and memory in both biological and artificial systems. Its principle of synaptic change driven by neuronal activity remains a key component in many models of neural processing and learning algorithms.
