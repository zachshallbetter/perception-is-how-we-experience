# Sensation

## **Neural coding**

Neural coding, also known as neural representation, is a field in neuroscience that studies the relationship between a stimulus and the electrical activity of neurons in the brain. It is based on the theory that the brain represents sensory and other information through networks of neurons, which can encode both digital and analog information.

A neural circuit, also known as a biological neural network, is a group of neurons interconnected by synapses and working together to perform a specific function. Multiple neural circuits connect to form large-scale brain networks. It is worth noting that although neural circuits have inspired the design of artificial neural networks, they have significant differences.

In the nervous system, a synapse is a structure that enables a neuron to transmit an electrical or chemical signal to another neuron or to the target effector cell.

Neurons are typically classified into three types based on their function. Sensory neurons respond to stimuli such as touch, sound, or light, and they send signals to the spinal cord or brain. Motor neurons receive brain and spinal cord signals to control muscle contractions and glandular output. Interneurons connect neurons to other neurons within the same brain or spinal cord region. When multiple neurons are functionally connected together, they form what is called a neural circuit.

Interneurons also referred to as internuncial neurons, relay neurons, association neurons, connector neurons, intermediate neurons, or local circuit neurons, are neurons that connect brain regions, i.e. not direct motor or sensory neurons. Interneurons are the central nodes of neural circuits, enabling communication between sensory or motor neurons and the central nervous system (CNS). They play vital roles in the adult mammalian brain's reflexes, neuronal oscillations, and neurogenesis.

### **Inputs**

The representative inputs for the Perceiver model encompass a broad spectrum of sensory data, each contributing to its comprehensive perceptual capabilities:

1. **Visual (Sight)**:
    - **Human Experience**: Vision involves perceiving light, colors, shapes, motion, and depth.
    - **Data Representation**: Captured through cameras and sensors, visual data includes images, videos, and spatial data. In IoT, devices like surveillance cameras, drones, and environmental sensors capture visual data for monitoring and analysis.
2. **Auditory (Hearing)**:
    - **Human Experience**: Hearing encompasses the perception of sounds, ranging from speech to noise.
    - **Data Representation**: Sound data is captured through microphones and audio sensors. This includes voice recordings, ambient sounds, and acoustic signals. IoT applications involve smart speakers, environmental noise monitoring, and voice-activated devices.
3. **Tactile (Touch)**: [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatosensory_system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatosensory_system)
    - **Human Experience**: Touch involves sensing pressure, texture, vibration, and temperature.
    - **Data Representation**: Haptic sensors, pressure sensors, and temperature sensors in IoT devices capture tactile data. Applications include touch-sensitive interfaces, wearables for health monitoring, and industrial automation systems.
4. **Olfactory (Smell)**: [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olfactory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olfactory)
    - **Human Experience**: Smell is the detection of airborne chemicals or particles.
    - **Data Representation**: Electronic noses and gas sensors mimic this sense, capturing data on air quality, detecting hazardous gases, or even assessing food freshness in smart appliances.
5. **Gustatory (Taste)**: [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustatory)
    - **Human Experience**: Taste discerns flavors and is closely linked with smell.
    - **Data Representation**: While challenging to replicate, electronic tongues or taste sensors are in development for applications like food quality analysis and chemical detection.
6. **Vestibular (Balance and Spatial Orientation)**: [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestibular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestibular)
    - **Human Experience**: This sense helps maintain balance and understand spatial orientation.
    - **Data Representation**: Gyroscopes and accelerometers in IoT devices capture motion and orientation data, used in smartphones, drones, and wearable fitness trackers.
7. **Proprioception (Body Position and Movement)**: [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprioception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprioception)
    - **Human Experience**: The sense of body position and movement, knowing where each part of the body is.
    - **Data Representation**: Motion sensors and wearable technology track body movements and positions, applicable in virtual reality systems, sports performance monitoring, and physical therapy.
8. **Interoception (Internal Body Sensation)**: [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interoception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interoception)
    - **Human Experience**: This includes sensing internal body states like hunger, thirst, and heart rate.
    - **Data Representation**: Wearable health monitors and medical IoT devices track heart rate, hydration levels, and other vital signs for health and fitness applications.
